## The Science: Why This Works

Your sleep‑wake cycle is governed by the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) — your brain's master clock. This clock is set by external cues called _zeitgebers_ (German for "time givers")[reference:2]. The most powerful zeitgebers are:

- **Light** — Morning bright light advances your clock; evening blue light delays it[reference:3]
- **Temperature** — A natural drop in core body temperature signals sleep onset[reference:4]
- **Meal timing** — When you eat tells your peripheral clocks when to expect activity or rest[reference:5]
- **Breath** — Controlled respiration down‑regulates the nervous system[reference:6]
- **Cognitive load** — Unprocessed thoughts keep your brain in high‑alert mode[reference:7]

This protocol systematically optimizes all five.

## Before You Begin: The Rules

1. **Anchor your wake time** — Pick a wake time and do not deviate for all 7 days[reference:8]
2. **No naps** — Sleep pressure is your ally in this reset
3. **Track your progress** — Log your sleep onset latency and wake‑after‑onset each morning[reference:9]
4. **If you slip** — Don't restart. Just pick up the next day and continue[reference:10]

## Day 1 — Set the Anchor

**Morning (within 30 minutes of waking):**

- Get outside into natural sunlight for 20–30 minutes. If it's overcast or you're up before sunrise, use a 10,000 lux light box positioned at eye level[reference:17].
- Keep indoor lights bright for the first 4 hours of your day.

**Afternoon:**

- Maintain moderate light levels. If you're indoors, sit near a window.

**Evening (2 hours before bed):**

- Turn off overhead lights. Switch to lamps with warm (2700K or lower) bulbs.
- Activate night mode on all screens. Better yet, put devices away entirely[reference:18].

**Evening (1 hour before bed):**

- **Cognitive off‑loading:** Take a piece of paper and write down every task, worry, or thought that's on your mind. Then fold it up and put it aside[reference:19]. This tells your brain: _"I've recorded this. I don't need to hold it."_

**Evening (30 minutes before bed):**

- **Breathwork:** Practice the 4‑7‑8 method — inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 7, exhale for 8. Repeat 4–6 cycles[reference:20].
- Set your thermostat to 65°F (18°C). If you have a smart mattress or cooling pad, activate the pre‑cooling cycle[reference:21].

**Bedtime:**

- Get into bed at your scheduled time. If you can't fall asleep within 20 minutes, get up, sit in dim light, and read a paper book until sleepy. Do not stay in bed awake.

---

## Day 2 — Reinforce the Wake Signal

**Morning:**

- Repeat the morning light exposure within 30 minutes of waking[reference:22].
- Consider adding a brief walk outdoors to combine light with movement.

**Throughout the day:**

- Keep your wake time consistent. No sleeping in.
- Expose yourself to as much natural light as possible.

**Evening:**

- Repeat the evening sequence from Day 1.
- **Add a warm foot bath or shower** 90 minutes before bed. Warming your extremities (hands and feet) helps your core temperature drop more efficiently, which is the primary signal for sleep onset[reference:23].

---

## Day 3 — Tighten the Meal Window

**Morning & Afternoon:**

- Continue the light and temperature protocols.
- **Pay attention to meal timing:** Have your last meal of the day at least 2–3 hours before bedtime[reference:24]. This allows your digestive system to quiet down before sleep.

**Evening:**

- If you feel hungry before bed, a small snack is acceptable — but keep it under 150 calories and avoid sugar and protein. A few crackers or a small banana works.

---

## Day 4 — Optimize the Wind‑Down

**Morning & Afternoon:**

- Continue all previous protocols.

**Evening:**

- **Extend your wind‑down** to a full 90 minutes. This is non‑negotiable.
- Dim lights progressively: at 90 minutes out, switch to 30 lux (think: a single warm lamp). At 60 minutes, drop to 10 lux (candlelight equivalent).
- No screens after the 90‑minute mark. If you must use a device, use red‑shift mode and keep brightness at minimum.

---

## Day 5 — Layer in Proprioceptive Cues

**Morning:**

- Continue morning light exposure.

**Evening:**

- **Add proprioceptive loading** — this is a fancy term for gentle physical input that grounds your nervous system. Try:
  - A 10‑minute gentle yoga or stretching session 90 minutes before bed
  - Progressive muscle relaxation: tense and release each muscle group from toes to scalp
  - A weighted blanket (if you have one)

**Bedtime:**

- Combine your breathwork with progressive muscle relaxation for maximum down‑regulation.

---

## Day 6 — Fine‑Tune Temperature

**Morning:**

- Continue all previous protocols.

**Evening:**

- **Dial in your temperature protocol:**
  - 90 minutes before bed: warm hands and feet (foot bath, warm socks, or a hot water bottle at your feet)
  - At bedtime: room at 65°F (18°C)
  - If you have a smart mattress or cooling device, set it to start cooling 30 minutes before bedtime[reference:25]

- The goal is to accelerate the natural 1–2°F drop in core body temperature that triggers sleep[reference:26].

---

## Day 7 — Consolidate and Lock In

**Morning:**

- By now, your wake time should feel natural. Continue the morning light exposure — this is now a permanent habit.

**Evening:**

- Run the full protocol one more time with precision:
  - 2 hours before bed: dim lights, blue‑light filters
  - 90 minutes: start wind‑down
  - 60 minutes: cognitive off‑loading
  - 30 minutes: breathwork + temperature prep
  - Bedtime: cool room, warm extremities

**Celebrate:** You've just completed a full circadian reset. Your sleep onset latency should be significantly reduced, and your sleep quality should feel deeper and more restorative.

---

## What to Do If You Slip

Life happens. If you miss a morning light session or stay up late one night:

1. **Do not panic** — one slip doesn't undo the protocol[reference:27]
2. **Maintain your anchor wake time** — this is the most important variable
3. **Pick up where you left off** the next day
4. **If you miss more than two days**, restart from Day 1
